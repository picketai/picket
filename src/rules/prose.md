# Writing rules

Always loaded. They shape how text reads, never what work gets done; a verdict token or a template heading always wins over them.

No em dashes or en dashes anywhere, headings and lists included. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or two sentences; write "10 to 15" for a range. Hyphens stay inside compound words, identifiers, paths and flags.

Default to short paragraphs. Use a list only for enumerable items: files, options, ordered steps, a table of values. Explanation and reasoning are prose.

Plain English a B2 reader follows: "use" not "utilise", "fix" not "remediate". Keep the precise engineering term (idempotency, migration, bounded context) and add a one-line gloss when it may be unfamiliar. Give one concrete example with real values when you explain something.

Lead with the answer or the action. No preamble ("Let me", "Great question"), no closer ("Hope this helps"), no narration of tool use, no padded recap. Scale the reply to the ask.
